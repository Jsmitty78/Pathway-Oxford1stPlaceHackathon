A standard walking frame that can see the floor in front of it.

Pathway clamps a repurposed old phone to an NHS walking frame and turns it into a fall-prevention system: it detects trip hazards on the floor, maps the rooms a user walks through, recognises a fall from the frame's angular acceleration, and pulls a caregiver into the loop when it matters.

A cost-driven, environmental mobility solution for an ageing population, built in 36 hours for a unit cost of £0.24. It took 1st place in a field of 115 participants.

              Reconstructed modules

              Core modules and their responsibilities
              ModuleResponsibility
              projectDetection Pinhole projection of world-space objects through the walker-mounted camera.
              classifyDetection Port of the original Python isHazard() frame-band admission rule.
              scoreHazards Ranks admitted hazards on proximity, path alignment, class severity and confidence.
              detectFallRisk Weighted six-signal fall scorer led by angular acceleration.
              planGuidance D.A.I. corridor search: steer around obstacles, step over low lips.
              triageEvent Blends motion and scene evidence into an escalation ladder.

              What the interviews changed

              • Most walker users stay indoors, and the biggest risks are trip hazards inside — so indoor hazard detection became the core of the product, not an add-on.
              • Caregivers want to be in the loop. That is why every event ends in a triage decision rather than a silent log.
              • Technology can be hard for elderly users. The on-device interface is one pill, one rail of large buttons, and a full-screen confirmation with a way out.
